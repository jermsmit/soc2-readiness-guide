# 2. Scoping and Choosing Type I vs Type II

**Status: outline, full content in progress**

## What this section will cover

- The real difference between Type I (design of controls at a point in time) and Type II (operating effectiveness over a period, typically 3 to 12 months)
- Why almost every serious enterprise or life sciences customer will eventually require Type II, and why starting with Type I is still often the right move
- How to define your system boundary: which products, environments, and data flows are in scope, and which are explicitly out
- Choosing your observation window and why the first Type II window is the highest-risk period in the whole program
- Setting a realistic timeline from kickoff to report delivery, including auditor lead time
- Life sciences note: handling multi-tenant platforms that serve both commercial and clinical trial data under different regulatory regimes within the same system boundary

## Common mistakes this section will address

- Scoping too broadly in year one and creating audit fatigue across teams that did not need to be involved
- Starting the Type II observation window before controls are actually operating consistently
